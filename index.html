How it works

Dewater designs a ring of dewatering wells around an excavation in an unconfined aquifer underlain by an impermeable layer. It is a quick screening tool: steady state, a uniform isotropic aquifer, fully penetrating wells, and the Dupuit assumptions.

The equation

One well pumping q from an unconfined aquifer lowers the head h at distance r to

H² − h² = (q / πk) · ln(R / r)

The governing equation is linear in h², so several wells add in H² − h², not in drawdown:

h = √( H² − Σ (qi / πk) · ln(R / ri) )

H is the initial saturated thickness, k the hydraulic conductivity, R the radius of influence, and ri the distance from well i. A well has no effect beyond R, and the head at a well is taken at its radius rw.

The least pumping rate

With the total rate Q shared equally by n wells, the head at each point inside the excavation is h = √(H² − (Q/n)·A), where A = Σ ln(R/ri)/(πk) depends only on where the wells are. The highest head is at the point with the least A, so the least Q that brings every point down to the target head is exact:

Q = n (H² − htarget²) / min A

Points are checked on a grid over the excavation and along its edge, where the water is usually highest. A design fails if that Q would pump a well dry (the head at its radius drops to zero): add wells or move them out.

Steps

  1. Find the site (search, or zoom and pan) and draw the excavation outline, drag a rectangle (Rotate turns it to match the site), or load a GeoJSON polygon.
  2. Enter the aquifer properties and the target head.
  3. Click to place wells, or generate a ring at an offset from the edge.
  4. Enter a pumping rate and check the contours, or find the least rate that works.
  5. Check a cross section: east–west or north–south through the excavation, or any line you draw. It shows the water level against the static level and the target, with the excavation and the wells on the line.
  6. Use the design curve to choose the number of wells.

The map shows head, drawdown, or head below the target as contours, and shades the area where the head is below the target. Save the project to come back to it; the file holds the outline, the wells, and the inputs.
